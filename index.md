## Research
- Thermodynamics of processes
- Non-Hermitian physics: A Hilbert space is vector space endowed with an inner product structure. Given a quantum system, it can be represented by a pure state and a set of operators on the Hilbert space. This representation is not unique, and one can have unitarily equivalent representations of the same quantum systems, which means that a quantum state ρ and the set of operators $A$ and the state UρU† with the set of operator {UAU†} 
(where U is a unitary operator satisfying U†U=1), correspond to the same quantum system. Note that one 
has the freedom to chose an inner product for the vector space, and this choice corresponds to the choice 
of the representation. Therefore, representations of quantum systems in non-Euclidean inner product 
spaces must also be unitarily equivalent. 
We consider a class of inner product spaces parametrized by the metric operator G, which is positive, 
bounded and Hermitian. Choosing G=1 gives us the usual quantum mechanics with Euclidean inner product 
space H, otherwise we get the pseudo-Hermitian quantum mechanics, with Hilbert space HG, where the 
operators with real eigenvalues satisfy the quasi-Hermiticity 
condition H†G=GH. The figure represents the isometric maps
